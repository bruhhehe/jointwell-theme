Facebook is where the customer is, daily. Everything else is secondary. The Page is the shop window; the Group is the identity the report says Jointwell is missing: a ritual, not a title. The ritual is the morning half hour.

## Facebook Group: The Morning Half Hour

- Name: The Morning Half Hour (Jointwell)
- Type: Private, visible. People have to ask to join, which keeps it small and honest and keeps the posts out of public search.
- Description: For people who put a Jointwell wrap on with the first cup of tea, and for anyone thinking about it. Post what the wrap went on today, ask questions, tell us what is wrong with it. Run by [FIRST NAME], who started this because of his grandad's mornings. Not a place for medical advice: that is your GP and physio. No selling other products.
- Join questions (max three): Do you have a Jointwell wrap yet? (Yes / Not yet / Thinking about it). Which joint runs your day? (Knee / Shoulder / Elbow / Other). Do you agree to the rules?
- Rules:
  1. First names only, and only your own story. No photos of other people without their say-so.
  2. No medical advice. "Ask your GP" is always the right answer. We will remove posts that tell someone what to do about a condition.
  3. Honest posts stay up, including the ones that say the wrap did not help. We will not delete criticism.
  4. No selling, no links to other products, no fundraising.
  5. Be kind. Everyone in here has a joint that hurts.
- Admin: the owner. One moderator from the first ten members who posts regularly, invited by message.

### Turning Group replies into on-page testimonials

Only with written permission, and only for what they actually wrote.

1. When a member posts something worth using, reply in the thread with thanks, then send a private message: "Would you be happy for us to put those words on the website, with your first name and age, exactly as you wrote them? If you have a photo of the wrap in your kitchen, that too, captioned 'photo supplied by customer'. Say no and nothing changes."
2. Keep the message and their reply as the permission record (screenshot into a folder named by first name and date).
3. Use the words verbatim. No tidying beyond removing a typo. Their first name, their age if they gave it.
4. Add the quote as a Judge.me review only if they submit it themselves through the review link; otherwise it goes on the page as "From the Morning Half Hour group, with permission", not as a verified review. Do not blur the two.
5. Never use a Group post that mentions a condition improving as if the wrap did it. "The stairs were easier" is fine. "My arthritis is better" is not, even if she said it, because on our page it becomes our claim.
